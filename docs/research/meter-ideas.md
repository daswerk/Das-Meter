# Research: Meter ideas from other metering tools

Researched 2026-10-20. This note collects what established metering and analysis tools offer that Das-Meter does not have yet, so that new Meters and Meter features can be picked from it. It names candidates and ranks them at the end, but it does not decide anything.

Terms follow [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md). "Has today" refers to [docs/site/src/meters.md](../site/src/meters.md) at `e68f4c1` (origin/main): Waveform, Spectrum, Spectrogram, Loudness Meter, Stereometer (Polar and Lissajous views), Phase Scope (with Overlay Source), Cepstrum and Oscilloscope.

**How sources were checked.** The research machine's proxy blocked every vendor site (minimeters.app, izotope.com, voxengo.com, youlean.co, tbproaudio.de, fabfilter.com, plugin-alliance.com, nugenaudio.com, hofa-plugins.de, meldaproduction.com, waves.com, bluecataudio.com, rme-audio.de, tcelectronic.com, flux.audio, masteringthemix.com, adptraudio.com, ableton.com, support.apple.com), and also kvraudio.com, lsp-plug.in, x42-plugins.com, Wikipedia and archive.org. Two kinds of source were used:

- **Source code**, read directly from shallow git clones of GitHub repositories at the commit named. These facts are verified.
- **Vendor pages**, known only from search-engine excerpts of the vendor's own pages and manuals. They are cited to the vendor page the excerpt came from and marked **(excerpt)**. They have not been read in full and should be re-checked before they are relied on.

Not checked at all: Sonarworks SoundID (a speaker/headphone calibration product, not a meter), JamesDSP and projectM (no search was made), TBProAudio mvMeter beyond one excerpt.

**Licences.** x42 meters.lv2 is GPL-2.0 and Friture and LSP Plugins are GPL-3.0 (their `COPYING` files). Per CLAUDE.md they are named here for ideas only; no code may be copied from them.

**Compute cost** is our own estimate, marked as such, on this scale: *cheap* = a few operations per sample or work Das-Meter already does (an FFT it already runs); *medium* = an extra FFT, filter bank or oversampler per Source; *heavy* = model-based analysis, long histories with search, or many Sources at once.

**Fit** says how the idea sits with what exists: analysers in `crates/analysis`, one Source per Meter (mono, Left/Right or Mid/Side Channel Views), the Phase Scope's Overlay Source as the only place two Send Plugins are analysed together, and the Send Plugin transport carrying DAW tempo and beat position.

## 3. Shortlist, ranked

Ranking is our own judgement: value to music producers, weighed against cost and how much existing code it reuses.

1. **Spectrum with a second Source and collision marks.** Extends the Overlay Source to the Spectrum. Pro-Q, Neutron and MMultiAnalyzer need inter-plugin links to do this; Send Plugins already give it to us.
2. **Multiband correlation / width per band** (a Stereometer view or a small Meter). HOFA, Metric AB, x42 and REFERENCE all have it; it is the Stereometer's maths run per band.
3. **Reference curve and long-term average on the Spectrum** (freeze/save a curve, compare live). The core of Tonal Balance Control and SPAN Plus static spectrums, using the FFT the Spectrum already runs.
4. **Clip and true-peak over counters** on the Loudness Meter. Cheap, universally useful, true peak is already computed (SPAN, WLM).
5. **Tuner view of the Cepstrum** (cents needle, pitch over time). Ableton and Melda ship tuners; the pitch finder exists.
6. **VU/PPM ballistics and K-System scales** for the Loudness Meter's bars (and a small VU Meter for the Bar). Standard in x42, Pro-L 2, Insight 2, MiniMeters.
7. **Loudness histogram and PSR/crest history** on the Loudness Meter. x42 and Youlean; views of numbers the Meter already has.
8. **Tempo detection for System Capture.** Would let the Phase Scope lock without tapping (Logic's BPM Counter shows the transient-based approach); medium cost, and beat phase is the hard part (guess).

Left out on purpose: intelligibility, codec preview and surround, which are heavy or off the music-producer focus; bit meter and sample histogram, which are niche debugging tools.
